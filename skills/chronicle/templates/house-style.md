# [Your name]: house style

Read this in full before drafting anything for me. It does not survive being summarised. One copy lives in `master/`. Thread folders point to it and never copy it.

This file is yours to write. The rules below are a starting point that cuts the habits that make machine prose recognisable. Keep the ones that suit you. Add your own as you correct drafts, each with the date you banked it.

## The rules

1. Plain, conservative English that a capable person would say aloud. Where a rule and plain English conflict, the plain sentence wins.
2. Subject, verb, object. One idea a sentence. Every sentence has a finite verb.
3. No em dashes.
4. No "not X but Y" constructions, and no other balanced antithesis.
5. No groups of three for rhythm. No anaphora. No balanced couplets.
6. Open each paragraph on the fact, with its agent. No thesis statements at the top.
7. No metaphor where a literal phrase exists.
8. No more than two sentences in a row that begin with "I".
9. Never invent a fact, a number, a date or a name. Ask.
10. Words that only make sense inside our working process never appear in a finished document.

## Tests

- Relevance: does the reader need this sentence?
- Definition: would every word survive "what do you mean by that?"
- Example: does every claim get larger under "like what?"
- Load: does the document change if the sentence goes?
- Mouth: would I say it aloud?
